Cross-vendor

The same training run, from chips of three makers.

A 138M-parameter GPT, twenty Adam steps, one hash per step. Apple, NVIDIA and plain CPUs produce the same bytes.

Float arithmetic is deterministic on one chip and famously not across chips: every vendor sums in its own order, fuses what it likes and approximates what it can. This page is a record of making it cross the line anyway, at real scale, with the real weights of a real model: the rules that did it, the records from every chip, the gate that compares them, the defects planted to prove the gate fails, and what the discipline costs against the vendors' own libraries. Nothing here is a claim about the model. It is a claim about replay: a verifier with any of these chips, or none, recomputes the producer's exact state.

The claim, measured

Twenty steps, six chips, one hash.

The model is Ledatic's own 138M GPT (16 pre-norm blocks, d=768, 12 heads, FF=3072, vocab 16384), loaded from its checkpoint with every parameter trained: embeddings, blocks, final LayerNorm, head. Each step is 512 tokens of real text as four windows of 128. After each step every one of the 197 tensors (555,669,504 bytes) is hashed (FNV-1a) and the hash is the record. The step-0 activations are hashed too, fifteen of them, so a defect is named by the first kernel it touches. Measured 2026-10-29.

37 PASS is one pair's full score: the inputs hash, fifteen activations, twenty steps, the final state. The loss is a human reading beside the hashes, not a gated one: cross-entropy 15.60 at step 0 to 9.34 at step 19 (the text is tokenized by a later tokenizer than the checkpoint's, so the model starts by learning a new vocabulary; the model's own corpus did not survive). A CPU replays the GPUs' trajectory byte for byte, sixteen times slower than the same machine's GPU: a verifier needs no GPU, only patience.

How

One arithmetic, written once, compiled three ways.

Every operation lives in one C header that the Metal shader, the CUDA kernel and the plain C program all compile from the same text. The rules, each one measured first on a probe before it was trusted: a fused multiply-add is written as an explicit fma and nothing else may be fused (Metal: #pragma METAL fp contract(off); nvcc: -fmad=false, which contracts across statements by default; hosts: -ffp-contract=off); every dot product is one sequential fma chain over its reduction axis ascending, the order Apple's matrix unit was measured to use, so CUDA cores reproduce the Apple matrix unit's bits; LayerNorm takes 1/sqrt, two correctly rounded operations on every vendor, never the hardware rsqrt; exp and tanh are fma polynomials, never the hardware builtins, which differ across vendors; division and square root are the IEEE ones, which agree. The step itself (forward, causal multi-head attention backward, embeddings, Adam over everything) is one shared orchestration that each backend includes, so the sequence of kernels cannot differ either.

The gate, and what was planted to break it

A gate that has never failed is a hope.

The gate takes the Mini GPU record as the reference and checks every other record against it, hash by hash. Before it was trusted, defects were planted in the real source, built by the real build script, run at the real configuration and judged by the real gate; the source was restored and compared byte for byte after each, and a control run passed.

The cost

What the discipline costs against the vendors' libraries.

At the model's GEMM shapes, C = A B was computed by the plain one-thread-per-output chain the run above uses, by a tiled kernel that keeps the same per-output chain (so the same bits), and by the vendor's library. Best of five, TFLOPS, every output hashed. On the NVIDIA A10:

The best deterministic kernel runs at about half of cuBLAS FP32 and a fifth of TF32 on the biggest shape, and worse where the tile count is small. cuBLAS FP32 happened to accumulate in the chain's order on three shapes of seven; TF32 differs everywhere it exists. On the Mac Mini M4 Pro the same comparison against Apple's MPS: the house's simdgroup kernel 2.84 TFLOPS against MPS 1.13 on the head shape, 2.56 against 4.41 at 2048 cubed, and MPS reproduced the chain's bits on all seven shapes at these sizes. The training run above does not yet use the tiled kernels; that is its next step, with the hashes required to stay.

Records

Every number above has a file.

The records are the programs' unedited output, one file per chip, with the device, compiler and flags in their first lines. The summary over all of them is signed by Ledatic's Mini witness key against a beacon pulse; the key's public half sits beside it.

To check a record against another, the gate is forty lines of shell: it compares every hash= field keyed by its line, in order, and exits nonzero on the first difference. To reproduce a record, the programs and the data manifest (sha256 of the checkpoint slice and the token windows) are in Ledatic's rail-lab repository under crossvendor/rungc.

Not claimed

What this does not say.

  • Nothing about the model's quality or its own training. The text is another tokenizer's, the loss starts at 15.6, the GELU is the tanh form and the checkpoint may have used the erf form.
  • Twenty steps, 512 tokens a step. Longer runs, bigger batches and other shapes are not measured.
  • Two NVIDIA generations and two Apple generations, two kinds of CPU. No AMD or Intel GPU, no Hopper, yet.
  • The run's kernels are the slow plain chain. The cost table says what a tiled deterministic kernel costs against the vendor libraries; the run's seconds are not that kernel's.
  • This is the craft half of the thesis, replayability across vendors. The signed ledger, provenance and retention, needs none of this and is the other page.
