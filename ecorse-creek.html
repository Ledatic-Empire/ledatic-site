Research notes · Wyandotte, Michigan

Ecorse Creek Fill

The idea being tested: Parks and yards along Ecorse Creek in Wyandotte, Ecorse and Lincoln Park were raised with the same contaminated fill, moved during repeated reshaping of the creek. This page sets that idea against the public record, one quote at a time.

Updated 18 November 202610 public documents28 quotes, each checked against its source

EPA cleaned up the North Drive yards by June 2003 (R3, p. 6). Its 2016 review found that "the remedy is protective of human health and environment" (R3, p. 6). The park's trash layer stays in place under clean fill, with a deed restriction on the land.

Where it stands

+The record supports

  • Industrial waste was used to fill wetland along North Drive in Wyandotte, between a 1945 aerial photo and a house built on the lot in 1955.
  • The creek there was reshaped at least twice: rechanneled by 1957, and its south bank straightened with construction debris in the early 1980s.
  • Cyanide waste similar to North Drive's turned up on a second Wyandotte lot, on Oak Street, about 1.5 miles away, in 1995.

−The record cuts against

  • The one park EPA dug into, Kiwanis 1000 Park, held household trash. EPA says it had no similarities to the waste in the yards and did not continue under the neighbouring lots.
  • No source has been found yet for contamination at Beaver, Lions, Pepper or Council Point parks.

?Not known yet

  • Which industry the waste came from and who hauled it. No document read so far names either.
  • What the fill north of the river is made of. EPA says only that it is extensive.
  • Anything about fill in Ecorse or Lincoln Park. Every document read so far is about Wyandotte or the whole watershed.

Two different fills on one site

On the one stretch of creek EPA dug into, the yards and the park next to them were raised with different material. The yards held the blue gas-cleaning waste. The park held household trash, and EPA found it stopped at the park's edge.

Both columns use the same scale, in feet below the surface. The yard column marks the depths where things were measured, not the thickness of a layer, which EPA does not give. Yards: N2, p. 1 for the blue soil on one lot, FR for the highest reading. Park: R3, p. 19.

Where EPA's own papers disagree

  • The site's EPA ID: MID985574227 on the review's summary form (R3, p. 7), MID980274179 on its map sheet (R3, p. 22).
  • Where Oak Street lies from North Drive: "about 1.5 miles southwest" (R3, p. 17), "approximately 1.5 miles west-northwest" (FR).

Still open

Questions

  • Which parks besides Kiwanis 1000 Park are said to sit on contaminated fill, in which city, and where that claim was first made. Named so far: Beaver, Lions, Pepper and Council Point.
  • Which industry made the blue waste, and who hauled it to North Drive.
  • How the same kind of waste reached a lot on Oak Street, 1.5 miles away.
  • What the extensive fill north of the river, visible by 1957, is made of.
  • Whether any state report from the 1920s to the 1950s describes dumping along the creek.

Records that could answer them

  • EPA Region 5: the Remedial Investigation (February 1996), and the two volumes of aerial photo interpretation and the 1990 extent-of-contamination study listed in the site's administrative record. These should hold the fill mapping.
  • The full text of EPA's Record of Decision, 17 July 1996. Only a catalogue entry has been found online.
  • ATSDR's public health assessment for the site, 29 November 1995.
  • Wayne County Drain Commissioner files for Ecorse Creek and the Sexton-Kilfoil and LeBlanc drains, 1920s to 1960s.
  • The State's Ecorse River Water Quality Study, August 1969.
  • Historical aerial photos (Wayne State University's collection) and the Wyandotte Herald (Library of Congress). Not yet opened.

How this page is made. Every quote is copied from a public document and linked to it. Before the page is built, a script checks each quote against the document's own text on the page cited, and a quote it cannot find stops the build. Where the document is a scan, the quoted pages were also compared with the page image. Words in square brackets replace a house number; the addresses are in the EPA records.

The documents were searched and read, and this page assembled, with the help of an AI model. The quote check above is what stands between that and the page: a quote reaches the page only if it is in the document.

This is a reading of public records, not the opinion of an engineer, a doctor or a lawyer. Where it says a document does not show something, it means a search did not find it, not that it never happened.
