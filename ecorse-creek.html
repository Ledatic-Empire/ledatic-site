Research notes · Wyandotte, Michigan

Ecorse Creek Fill

Are four parks along Ecorse Creek built on the same contaminated fill as the yards of the North Drive cyanide site in Wyandotte? No test of the parks' soil has been found, so the public record cannot say yes or no. It does show why the question is fair, what counts against it, and what would settle it.

The idea, as it was put to us: Parks and yards along Ecorse Creek in Wyandotte, Ecorse and Lincoln Park were raised with the same contaminated fill, moved during repeated reshaping of the creek.

The short version

In plain words, one statement to a line. After each one, a date jumps down this page to the quote it rests on; a source key such as AS, p. 17 opens the document itself.

What happened on North Drive

Between 1945 and 1955, low, swampy ground by the creek on North Drive was filled with material from local industries. 1945 to 1955

People who lived nearby during the filling remember the blue soil being called "gas wash". 1945 to 1955

In 1989, workers digging on one of the lots found blue soil. EPA found a large area of soil contaminated with ferric ferrocyanide, a cyanide compound, and suspected it came from a plant that made gas from coal. 1989, R3, p. 18

EPA dug contaminated soil out from around the houses, finished its cleanup by 2003, and in 2005 took the site off the National Priorities List, its list of Superfund cleanup sites. R3, p. 18, R3, p. 6, FR

Residents have reported digging up blue soil in their yards since. EPA's 2021 and 2026 reviews rate the cleanup "Short-term Protective", and the 2026 review says more steps are needed for it to be protective in the long term. September 2007, January 2021, R5, p. 15, January 2026

Why people ask about the parks

EPA's 1990 study of old aerial photos says much of the creek's wetland and banks in Wyandotte was filled between the 1930s and 1966, and that the filled land went to industry, houses and recreation. 1930s to 1966, By 1966

In 1993, EPA wrote that interviews with former residents indicated the same material may have been used as fill all along the creek. 24 November 1993

In 1994, a caller told EPA of other places where blue material had been seen: Wyandotte, Ecorse and Lincoln Park. 15 August 1994

The aerial study saw mounded fill that may be mixed with refuse on the west side of the creek's South Branch, north of Goddard Road, in 1951. 1951

In 1971 and 1978 it saw what appeared to be waste disposal at and west of the point where the creek's two branches meet. 1971, 1978

Lions Park is on that west bank north of Goddard Road, Beaver Park is beside Goddard Road, and Council Point Park is where the branches meet. Lions Park, Beaver Park, Council Point Park

The study's second volume, its aerial photos, was available at EPA's Region 5 office in 1990. AS, p. 17

They have not been found in EPA's online record of the site. what was read

What counts against it

At Kiwanis 1000 Park on North Drive, EPA's digging in 2000 found a layer of household trash with no similarities to the cyanide waste, and the layer did not continue under the neighbouring lots. R3, p. 17, R3, p. 19, March 2000

That trash did hold lead and arsenic slightly above state cleanup standards, and EPA's 2026 review describes a clean fill cover above it. R3, p. 19, R5, p. 15

The aerial study saw few "suspicious" materials, such as drums, debris and rubbish, in the fill. October 1990

Those reports came from interviews. EPA wrote at the time that its data covered only "the area of the removal action". That was its cleanup around the houses, which began that month. 24 November 1993, R3, p. 18

What is missing

No soil test has been found for Beaver, Lions, Pepper or Council Point park. what was read

Nothing found so far describes fill near Pepper Park, on the creek's North Branch. what was read

At a public meeting in 1996, EPA said it would be impossible to sample every lot in the city, and asked anyone who saw suspicious colored soil to tell it. TR, p. 37

Who made the waste is not settled. EPA named two suspects in 1993, the gas company now called MichCon and BASF, and its own memo says only anecdote linked either one to the site. Both companies refused EPA's offer to carry out the investigation. 24 November 1993, 22 December 1993

What would settle it

Soil tests in the four parks would settle it.

Short of tests, three things could narrow it: the aerial study's volume of photos, the cities' records of how each park was built, and any sampling by EPA, the state or the cities that this search has not found.

The evidence, in full

Everything above rests on what follows: where the record stands, every dated statement in order, and the documents themselves. EPA's papers call the creek the Ecorse River.

Where it stands

+The record supports

  • Industrial waste was used to fill wetland along North Drive in Wyandotte, between a 1945 aerial photo and a house built on the lot in 1955.
  • EPA's 1990 study of aerial photos says much of the creek's wetland and banks in Wyandotte were filled between the 1930s and 1966, and that the filled land went to industrial, residential and recreational uses.
  • The same study saw possible refuse in fill along the South Branch in 1951 and 1957, and waste disposal west of and at the point where the branches meet in 1971 and 1978.
  • In 1993 EPA recorded former residents saying the same waste may have been used as fill all along the creek, and reports of other filled areas in Wyandotte and other communities. These are reports, not tests.
  • Cyanide waste similar to North Drive's turned up on a second Wyandotte lot, on Oak Street, about 1.5 miles away, in 1995.
  • The creek there was reshaped at least twice: rechanneled by 1957, and its south bank straightened with construction debris in the early 1980s.

−The record cuts against

  • Kiwanis 1000 Park on North Drive, where EPA dug in 2000, held household trash. EPA says it had no similarities to the waste in the yards and did not continue under the neighbouring lots.
  • The aerial study saw few drums, debris or rubbish in the fill it watched go in.
  • No test result has been found for Beaver, Lions, Pepper or Council Point park.

?Not known yet

  • Who made the blue waste. EPA named two suspects in 1993, the gas company now called MichCon and BASF, owner of the former Michigan Alkali. Its own memo says nothing but anecdote linked either to the site, and both refused to do the investigation.
  • Whether the fill the aerial study saw lies under today's parks. The study's annotated photos are not online.
  • What the fill north of the river is made of. EPA found pockets of contaminated fill on the north bank, and construction debris and foundry sand in borings near the river.
  • Fill in Ecorse or Lincoln Park. A caller told EPA in 1994 he had seen blue material in both, without saying where; no test there has been found. EPA said in 1996 it could not sample every lot in the city.

Two different fills on one site

On the one stretch of creek EPA dug into, the yards and the park next to them were raised with different material. The yards held the blue gas-cleaning waste. The park held household trash, and EPA found it stopped at the park's edge.

Both columns use the same scale, in feet below the surface. The yard column marks the depths where things were measured, not the thickness of a layer, which EPA does not give. Yards: N2, p. 1 for the blue soil on one lot, FR for the highest reading. Park: R3, p. 19.

Where EPA's own papers disagree

  • The site's EPA ID: MID985574227 on the review's summary form (R3, p. 7), MID980274179 on its map sheet (R3, p. 22).
  • Where Oak Street lies from North Drive: "about 1.5 miles southwest" (R3, p. 17), "approximately 1.5 miles west-northwest" (FR).
  • When the filling began: "Before 1930, land along the banks of the Ecorse River in Wyandotte was primarily wetlands" (R3, p. 18); "Between the 1930's and 1966" (AS, p. 6); "Beginning in the 1920s and continuing to the 1980s" (R4, p. 4). The 2021 and 2026 reviews give no source for the earlier date.

Still open

Questions

  • Whether the fill and possible waste the 1990 aerial study saw along the South Branch and west of where the branches meet, 1951 to 1978, lies under Beaver, Lions or Council Point park. The study's annotated photos would show it.
  • What the letter of 30 December 1952 in the Detroit Mayor's Papers says about a dump northwest of the North Drive site.
  • Where in Ecorse and Lincoln Park the 1994 caller saw blue material.
  • What BASF and Michigan Consolidated Gas told EPA after it named them in 1993.
  • Who owned and used the land west of where the branches meet while the study saw waste disposal there. Council Point Park's land was bought from the Levy Company in the late 1980s, according to Wikipedia's entry on the park, which cites no source for it.
  • How the same kind of waste reached a lot on Oak Street, 1.5 miles away, and when. EPA said in 1996 that it did not know when Oak Street was filled.
  • Whether any state report from the 1920s to the 1950s describes dumping along the creek.

Records that could answer them

  • EPA Region 5: Volume 2 of the 1990 aerial study, the annotated photos, which the study says is kept at the Region V office.
  • The Detroit Mayor's Papers, 1952, Box 5, Department of Public Works file: the letter of 30 December 1952, as a caller described it to EPA in 1994.
  • EPA's enforcement file for the site: the 1993 notice letters to BASF and Michigan Consolidated Gas, their replies, and the earlier letters asking about past practices.
  • Wyandotte's, Lincoln Park's and Ecorse's own park records for these four parks.
  • Wayne County Drain Commissioner files for Ecorse Creek and the Sexton-Kilfoil and LeBlanc drains, 1920s to 1960s.
  • The State's Ecorse River Water Quality Study, August 1969.
  • Historical aerial photos (Wayne State University's collection) and the Wyandotte Herald (Library of Congress). Not yet opened.
